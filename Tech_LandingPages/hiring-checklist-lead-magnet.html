A 12-point checklist for tech leaders competing for senior engineers, product leads, and designers in a market where the office is part of the pitch.

01 Focus & Deep Work

Senior engineers and product leads will immediately clock whether they can do their best work here. They've seen too many offices not to.

There is dedicated quiet space where deep work is actually possible Senior technical candidates will ask about this. Have an answer they can see for themselves.
The desk setup is noticeably better than home Monitor size, chair quality, keyboard — not just "a desk."
Natural light reaches most workstations Or lighting has been deliberately designed, not defaulted.
02 Collaboration & Energy

Candidates evaluate whether this is a place where good work happens — or just where people sit. The difference is visible.

There are informal collision points Areas where people naturally run into each other — not just scheduled meeting rooms.
There is a range of collaboration spaces beyond the boardroom Standing tables, lounge areas, open project zones.
The office feels alive at baseline occupancy Not echoing and empty when half the team is remote that day.
03 Comfort & Amenities

These are the details candidates notice and factor in — even when they don't say it explicitly in the debrief.

Temperature and acoustics are controlled Your team isn't complaining about noise or cold every week.
There is access to good coffee and food On-site or close enough that people actually use it, not just endure it.
People have somewhere to put their things Lockers, storage, personal space — not hauling a bag every time they come in.
04 Design & Signal

This is what closes or loses the hire. The design tells candidates exactly what kind of company they'd be joining.

The space makes candidates want to work there Not "this is fine" — but "I want to be in this office every day." That's the feeling that closes.
Candidates walk in and feel like the company has figured itself out That confidence is contagious. It signals leadership, vision, and that this is a place worth joining.
There is at least one detail candidates mention when they accept the offer The best offices become part of the hiring story. Candidates tell their network about the space.

How to read your score

10 – 12 Your office is doing active work in hiring. Candidates are choosing you partly because of the space.
7 – 9 You're competitive, but there are gaps that cost you candidates in close decisions.
4 – 6 Your office is likely neutral to negative in hiring conversations. You're working harder than you should to close.
0 – 3 Your office is costing you hires. In a competitive talent market, that's a significant and fixable problem.
